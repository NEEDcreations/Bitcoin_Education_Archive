Anonymous live-viewer presence. To show the live viewer counter on each channel, your browser writes a small presence record to our database every ~30 seconds containing only: (1) the channel id you are currently watching, and (2) a server timestamp. For signed-in users this record is keyed by your auth UID; for anonymous users it is keyed by a randomly-generated local id (stored in your browser's localStorage as tctv_viewerId). The record is deleted when you leave Timechain TV or close the tab, and automatically expires after ~65 seconds of inactivity. No IP, location, or identifying information is stored in the presence record.

Watch-time tracking. Minutes you watch are counted in this browser for badges. Signed-in viewers also add one minute at a time to a site-wide watch counter that is not labeled with your name. The first time you tune a channel on a given day, we may store a view record with the channel id, the day, and your viewer id. Clear local watch time from your browser settings. Presence heartbeats are described above and expire on their own.

Daily watch-reward heartbeat. If you are signed in and use the daily "Watch 10 minutes of Timechain TV" challenge, the app sends a heartbeat about once a minute while the YouTube player is playing and this tab is visible. The server stores one ledger for your account (tctv_watch/{uid}) with: the reward day (the UTC date, with the day changing at 5:00 AM UTC), credited seconds for that day (never more than 600), the server time of the last credited heartbeat, the day the reward was granted, and whether the first reward notice was already shown. We store this so we can grant 100 XP and 1 orange ticket once per reward day, and so your browser cannot invent the elapsed time, the day, or the reward. Each heartbeat can add at most about one minute, measured on the server. A pause, a hidden tab, a closed player, or a gap longer than about three minutes does not fill in the missing time. Other open tabs share the same ledger and do not add extra credit. You can read your own progress. Only the server can write the ledger. It is separate from the short-lived live-viewer presence record above, and separate from the localStorage and cumulative watch time used for badge tracking.

Push notifications

If you turn notifications on, we store a push token for that device on your account. Each device has its own token, and we keep at most 10 devices. The token is used only to deliver the categories you leave on. Turn notifications off anytime in Settings → Notifications, or in your browser or phone settings. Signing out removes this device's token, so the next person on a shared phone does not receive your notifications. If a device already had a push token and you had not turned notifications off, that registration stays on.

4. Data Storage

Server-side data is stored in Google Firebase (Firestore), hosted in the United States. Firebase is operated by Google and subject to Google's enterprise security practices and certifications.

Client-side data is stored in your browser's localStorage and in the PWA's service worker cache. This data stays on your device and is never transmitted to us.

Direct messages and forum posts are stored in Firebase Firestore. Messages are not end-to-end encrypted. We may access message content when investigating abuse reports.

Cookies

9. Content Moderation & Filtering

All user-generated content (Global Chat messages, forum posts, DMs, usernames, articles) is filtered client-side before submission. No content is sent to external moderation services. Usernames are checked again on the server before they are reserved. Filters include:

  • Profanity filter — 100+ blocked words with leet-speak normalization (e.g., "f*ck", "sh1t" detected)
  • Hate speech & racial slurs — 27+ racial/ethnic slurs blocked across all surfaces
  • Sexual content — 30+ explicit terms and phrase patterns blocked
  • Self-harm detection — phrases indicating self-harm intent are blocked in public chat and flagged in DMs
  • Threat detection — violent threats ("I will kill you", "I know where you live") blocked via regex patterns
  • Political & religious extremism — political party names, extremist slogans, and religious hate terms blocked in public spaces
  • PII protection — phone numbers and email addresses are hard-blocked in Global Chat (allowed in DMs with confirmation)
  • Link filtering — URLs are stripped from Global Chat messages
  • Spam detection — ALL CAPS, repeated characters, and excessive links are flagged
  • Username validation — ASCII-only enforcement prevents Unicode/homoglyph bypass attempts

Filtering for Global Chat, PlebTalk, DMs, usernames, and articles is performed in your browser. No message content is sent to external moderation APIs or third-party services. Blocked messages are never stored in Firebase.

Topic discussion comments are checked again on the server before they are stored. Hate, scams, spam, shortened links, and invite links are rejected and not stored. Other links are replaced, and ordinary profanity is masked. A question that Nacho answers is sent to the Cloudflare Workers AI proxy described in Section 8, as question text plus a topic excerpt only.

Firestore Data Structure

Sats claim data is stored in the following Firebase subcollections under your user document:

  • sats_withdrawals — individual claim records (amount, truncated invoice, timestamp, status)
  • sats_daily — daily aggregate tracking (amount claimed per day, claim count)
  • daily_points — server-side daily XP tracking (total awarded per day; full XP through 500, half rate through 900, then quarter rate until 1,000, floor per tier, nothing banked)
  • faucet_stats — global faucet statistics (total distributed, active claimants)
  • faucet_ip_log — HMAC of the claim IP (not the raw IP) at claim time, to detect multi-account abuse per network. Server-only.
  • faucet_subnet_log — HMAC of the claim network range. Server-only. The raw range is not stored.
  • ip_reputation — IPQualityScore verdict for an HMAC of an IP, kept 7 days (24 hours when the score is high). Server-only.
  • device_links and device_holds — HMAC of a server-verified device id, the accounts seen on it, and a hold or household approval. Kept 180 days. Server-only.
  • referral_signals — an HMAC of the IP captured when a referral is registered, plus the IPQualityScore verdict and device HMAC, and a 90-day expiry. Server-only. Not readable by other users. Deleted when the account-delete function runs.
  • faucet_fingerprints — device fingerprint hashes (detects multi-account abuse per device). This browser-made hash is a weak signal. The device id used for reward holds is the IPQualityScore id above.

The withdrawal list and daily totals stored on your profile are deleted with the account. The faucet ledger, invoice records, IP log, and device-fingerprint log are kept. Section 11 explains why.

Privacy note: We intentionally do NOT store full Lightning invoices. Only the first 50 characters are retained (followed by "...") for basic audit purposes. This means we cannot reconstruct the full payment destination after the claim is processed.
